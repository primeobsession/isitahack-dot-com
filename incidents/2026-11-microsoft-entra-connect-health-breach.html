How much do you want to read?

The latest · Nov 21, 2026, 1:17 PM ET

Microsoft says attackers exploited a Connect Health flaw to read tenant metadata; who and how many are not established.

How big is it?

Large. A worldwide cloud service, but Microsoft has not said how many organizations were affected, so this size is a provisional estimate. More than a dozen administrators reported getting the notice, per one outlet's description of a social media thread.

Who is affected?

Organizations that received Microsoft's notice through Azure Service Health, per Microsoft. It says they need no software update, and advises watching for phishing aimed at exposed administrator emails.

Known so far

  • Microsoft says unauthorized parties read tenant-level metadata through a flaw in the Entra Connect Health API. evidence
  • It says the flaw was introduced Jul 23 and discovered Nov 9. evidence
  • It says a server-side fix went live Nov 19 and no customer update is needed. evidence
  • It says it notified affected organizations on Nov 20. evidence
  • It says the exposed data included administrator and technical-contact emails and tenant configuration metadata. evidence
  • It says no passwords, tokens or valid keys were exposed (Microsoft's statement only). evidence

Not known yet

  • Who exploited the flaw.
  • How many organizations were affected.
  • When unauthorized access began.
  • Whether the exposed emails have been used in phishing.
  • Whether Microsoft's statement that no passwords, tokens or keys were exposed holds up.

Not verified: initial investigation only. Nothing on this page is absolutely confirmed. It is an early look based only on publicly available sources, and it should not be treated as verified fact. Even items labeled "Confirmed" only mean a public source reports them.
