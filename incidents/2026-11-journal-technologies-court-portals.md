### Known so far

- Journal Technologies says suspicious, malicious automated activity targeted its public portal software, first identified on Nov 13.
- The vendor advised customers to take their public portals offline, and courts did.
- Public portals or e-filing were offline at many courts, including Alameda, Kern, Marin, Ventura, Riverside, Los Angeles, Spokane and Lake County, Illinois.
- Kern's court says its portal is offline and gives no estimated restoration time.

### Not known yet

- Who was behind the activity.
- Whether any data was exposed; the vendor says it has found no evidence so far for Criminal Justice Information and is still investigating.
- When every affected portal will be restored, and how many courts are affected.
- Whether this is connected to the other outages reported this week.

## What we checked and did not find

We looked for these and did not find them as of our Nov 21 mid-morning check.

- A count or list of the courts and customers affected, from the vendor or any government body. The Sacramento Bee's count covers California superior courts only.
- A description of the flaws, a CVE, or indicators of compromise from the vendor.
- A claim of responsibility or any named attacker. We did not look at leak sites, underground forums or trackers.
- An FBI, CISA or court statement tying this incident to ARTEX or to anyone else.
- A finding on whether any data other than Criminal Justice Information was exposed.
- A list of courts whose portals are restored, or a restoration date for Kern.
- Any source linking this outage to the other outages reported this week.
- A vendor named in the Court of Appeal of Alberta's own notice; it says only “unscheduled maintenance.”
- A public vendor post newer than Nov 20.
- A vendor or cause named in the Washoe County, Nevada court's own notice; it says only that the portal is down with no ETA.
