Was the Together AI prompt leak a hack?

Also reported as the Together AI security event and the Together AI prompt leakage.

Is it a hack? The short answer

Confirmed

Yes, by the company's own statement: an unauthorized party used an exposed credential to download stored prompt samples and then tested credentials found in them. The company has not named who did it.

Rests on An unauthorized party got into Together AI's stored prompt samples · A credential committed by an AI coding agent opened a private repository · The party searched the prompts for credentials and tested them

This is the current answer, not a final verdict. A final verdict is published only after the site's owner has reviewed the evidence.

What happened?

4
ConfirmedThe samples were test prompts from June and July 2026Together AI says the stored samples came from requests to three models, mainly on June 24, July 4, July 8 and July 14, 2026.

Why it supports The company states the dates, models and purpose of the stored samples.

The company says it collected prompt samples authorized for routine software testing and stored them in a private repository. The models named are MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6.

  • Together AI blog: Security event: Prompt leakage for requests in June and July of 2026 (company statement) Official source · Nov 21, 2026

Last checked Nov 22, 2026, 1:52 AM ET

ConfirmedThe party searched the prompts for credentials and tested themTogether AI says the unauthorized party searched the samples for credentials to other services and tested whether they worked.

Why it supports The company's post describes the searching and testing, and the five canary credentials.

Five of the credentials tested were canary credentials (fake bait keys) from a security experiment run by Dragos. The company does not say whether any real customer credentials were tested or used.

  • Together AI blog: Security event: Prompt leakage for requests in June and July of 2026 (company statement) Official source · Nov 21, 2026

Last checked Nov 22, 2026, 1:52 AM ET

Why it supports The archived copy of the post shows the sentence; the current post does not.

The company has not said why it removed the sentence. No public source now says whether the data was deleted or passed on. Some news reports, including The Hacker News, still repeat the original line.
