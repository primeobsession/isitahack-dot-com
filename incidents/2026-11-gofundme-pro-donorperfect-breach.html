Were GoFundMe Pro and DonorPerfect hacked?

Also reported as GoFundMe Pro data breach, DonorPerfect cyberattack and Nonprofit fundraising platform attacks.

Is it a hack? The short answer

Confirmed

Yes, by both companies' own accounts: GoFundMe says an unauthorized party accessed GoFundMe Pro data; DonorPerfect says it found unauthorized activity and went offline. Who did it, and whether one attacker, is not established.

Rests on GoFundMe says an unauthorized third party accessed data from the GoFundMe Pro API on Oct 10 to 18 and on Nov 8 to 9, 2026 · DonorPerfect says it found unauthorized activity on its systems and took DonorPerfect offline · DonorPerfect attributes its attacks to AI bots, entering through its marketing website · The same attacker hit both GoFundMe Pro and DonorPerfect · Who is behind the attacks

This is the current answer, not a final verdict. A final verdict is published only after the site's owner has reviewed the evidence.

04

What happened?

3
ConfirmedGoFundMe Pro's connection to Salesforce was cut off, and GoFundMe says it remains disabledGoFundMe's status page reports data failing to sync to Salesforce from Nov 9.

Why it supports GoFundMe's own status page (Tier 1) gives the dates and current state.

On Nov 20 it says the GoFundMe Pro packages in Salesforce were restored but "the connection between GoFundMe Pro and Salesforce remains disabled" and customers should not reconnect yet. The status page does not itself say the shutdown was a security step; Salesforce's own advisory (c007) does, and on Nov 20 Salesforce also says the connected apps remain disabled.

Last checked Nov 21, 2026, 12:53 PM ET

ConfirmedSalesforce says it disabled the GoFundMe Pro connected app over suspicious use of access tokens, and that this was not a flaw in SalesforceSalesforce says on its own public status page that it disabled the GoFundMe Pro connected app as a precaution after suspicious use of access tokens, and that the problem was not a flaw in Salesforce.

Why it supports Salesforce's own public status advisory (Tier 1) states what it did and why; The NonProfit Times quotes the same statement.

Salesforce's Trust status advisory, posted Nov 11 (12:27 PM ET), says it "disabled the GoFundMe Pro connected app and associated managed packages as a precaution after identifying suspicious use of access tokens associated with the third-party provider" and that "the issue did not arise from a vulnerability within the core Salesforce platform." The same words were quoted in The NonProfit Times. In an update on Nov 20 (4:30 PM ET), Salesforce says it re-enabled the GoFundMe Pro managed packages, but the connected apps stay disabled until GoFundMe completes security remediation and shows the integrations can be restored safely. Salesforce says it will notify affected customers "without undue delay if we identify unauthorized access to their data"; it has not said it found any. That it was not a Salesforce flaw is Salesforce's own account; we found no independent review.
