ConfirmedRakuten Securities says information held in its test environment may have been leaked to an outside party on Nov 11The notice says its investigation found that on Wed, Nov 11, some information managed inside its test environment system may have leaked outside the company.

Why it supports The company's own notice (Tier 1) says this, and two outlets report it; the label records that the company says it, not that an outsider verified the timing.

The company words this as a possible leak (a risk of leakage), not a confirmed copy. It says it has finished identifying the affected customers and will tell them individually. Nikkei xTECH (Nov 18) reports the company told it that, after the Nov 17 notice, it confirmed the data had actually leaked; the notice itself still says possible leak. J-CAST News (Nov 18) reports the company told it the same. This is the company's account; no outside party has checked it.

ReportedThe company reportedly said that real customer data had been copied, unmasked, to the test environment during a system upgradeITmedia NEWS reports the company told it that part of its real customer data was copied to a test server for a set period during an upgrade without being masked; BreachNews reports the same.

Why it supports One Tier 2 outlet (ITmedia NEWS) quotes the company directly; BreachNews appears to relay the same comments. Later Tokyo Shimbun and TV Asahi reports conflict, and the company's own notices do not say it.

ITmedia NEWS (Nov 18) says the company answered its questions directly: data was copied from production to a test server to check some functions of an internal system upgrade, it was not masked, the step went through formal internal approval and was not one employee's decision, and the company normally keeps only dummy data in test systems. The company declined to say when the data was moved or how long the upgrade work ran. BreachNews (Tier 3) reports the same comments and likely relies on the same reporting, so the two count as one source. Tokyo Shimbun (Nov 21) and TV Asahi (Nov 20) instead report the company says whether the data was masked is still under investigation. Because the accounts conflict and the company's own notices do not address masking, this stays Reported.

ConfirmedRakuten Securities says Japan's Financial Services Agency ordered it on Nov 19 to report on the incidentThe company's Nov 19 release says it received an order on Nov 19 under Article 56-2(1) of the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act and Article 146(1) of the Personal Information Protection Act to report on the incident.

Why it supports A Tier 1 company release says it received the order, the Finance Ministry's published press-conference summary records the minister confirming it, and several Japanese outlets report it independently.

Yomiuri and Nippon TV report the order; Nippon TV says the financial services minister confirmed it and called it very regrettable, saying the agency would verify the report and respond strictly. Yomiuri says the agency judged the order necessary because of the scale and sensitivity of the data. NHK, Sankei and Tokyo Shimbun also report the order and the minister's comments. ITmedia NEWS (Nov 20) reports the minister said the agency is working closely with the police and Japan's National Cybersecurity Office, and has asked the company to check any reported fraudulent trade at once and report it to the agency; the Finance Ministry's own published summary of the Nov 20 press conference records these remarks, adds that the minister was told no fraudulent trades have been found so far, and says the agency will also check how the company stored customer data. The Asahi Shimbun (Nov 19) calls the order unusual for a financial firm hit by unauthorized access and reports that the agency will consider administrative action, such as a business improvement order, if the report shows flaws in how customer data was managed; Tokyo Shimbun reports the same expectation. That is the outlets' account of the agency's plans; no such action has been announced. We did not find a Financial Services Agency page on the order itself.
