# Was the Wellington IT credit union breach a hack?

**Confirmed.** Yes: an outside party got unauthorised access to saved-payee details on Wellington IT's banking platform, per the department, Wellington IT and the credit union league. Money taken and the attacker are not established.

## At a glance

- Status: monitoring
- Size: Large. About 40 to 43 Irish credit unions, roughly a quarter of the country's, are affected; outlets say potentially hundreds of thousands of app users. No exact count yet.
- Who is affected: Members of about 40 Irish credit unions who use the credit union banking app or online banking. Their saved payees' details were exposed; their own balances and logins were not, per Wellington.

### Known so far

- An outside party got unauthorised access to saved-payee details on Wellington IT's platform.
- Only payee details for users of credit union mobile banking apps are involved, per the Department of Finance.
- Payee names, IBANs, BICs, addresses and payment references may have been accessed, per a credit union's own notice.
- Wellington IT says the vulnerability is closed.
- The Department of Finance first learned of the incident on Nov 17.
- Wellington IT spotted the possible breach on Nov 15 and told credit unions on Nov 16.

## What we checked and did not find

We looked for these and did not find them as of the time of this report on Nov 22, 2026.

- Wellington IT's own notice to credit unions or to the public. We read it only through outlets, officials and one credit union's notice.
- The exact number of members, accounts or payees affected.
- Any technical detail on the flaw beyond Wellington's reported description of an 'authorisation flaw'.
- Any claim of responsibility, or any attacker named. Extortion emails were reported, but not who sent them.
- Any report that money was taken or that the data was used in fraud.
- A finding from the Data Protection Commission or the UK Information Commissioner's Office.
- The outside review's report.
