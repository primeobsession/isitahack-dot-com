Was the Expedia data breach a hack?

Also reported as the Expedia supply chain attack and the Expedia data leak.

Is it a hack? The short answer

Confirmed

A vendor-side attack, yes: Expedia confirmed, per Skift, that attackers got in through a compromised third-party data platform provider. What was taken is not established.

Rests on Expedia confirmed that attackers reached its systems through a compromised third-party data platform provider · Expedia says it contained the issue, is investigating with outside experts, and that its platforms remain safe

This is the current answer, not a final verdict. A final verdict is published only after the site's owner has reviewed the evidence.

Time of publication, converted from UTC.

Why it supports Two outlets' descriptions of the posted files; neither can show the files came from Expedia, and the company has not confirmed them.

These are Cybernews's descriptions of the files. Cyber Daily separately says the posted archive appears to be an extract, with 11 folders listing hundreds of thousands of email addresses linked to travel data such as log-ins, hotel stays and loyalty points, and that the data may be old given the date ranges in some files. It says some of the emails appear in earlier breaches and many do not. Expedia has not confirmed the files are genuine. Cybernews wrote on Nov 17 that it had not heard back from Expedia. Bcrypt hashes make cracking harder but not impossible, and Cybernews warns old accounts could be targeted.

What we checked and did not find

We looked for these and did not find them as of 3:31 PM ET on Nov 22, 2026.

  • An Expedia newsroom page or customer notice. The statement appears only as quotes to Skift and The Seattle Times.
  • The name of the vendor, or how the attacker got in.
  • Expedia saying whether customer data was taken, or confirming the posted files are real.
  • Any independent check of the group's claimed volume. Cyber Daily reports the group claimed more than one terabyte, and Cybernews says it claimed a billion records.
  • Any regulator filing or customer notice. Expedia's SEC filings show no Form 8-K under Item 1.05, the item for a material cyber incident; its latest 8-K, on Nov 4, is about earnings.
  • Any public statement from Qubole or its parent company, Idera.
  • Any link, confirmed by Expedia or researchers, between this breach and the Qubole attack.

Part of the Skift article is behind a subscription; we read the start of Expedia's quote and Skift's summary. We did not visit the group's leak page.
