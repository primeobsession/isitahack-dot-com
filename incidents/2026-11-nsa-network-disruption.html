The latest · Nov 20, 2026, 4:00 PM ET

The NSA says it is back to normal operations; the cause and answer are unchanged.

Is it back?

Back. The NSA says it is back to normal operations as of Nov 20. evidence Checked Nov 24, 2026, 11:20 AM ET

How big is it?

Nation-level potential. Potential only: it would be nation-level if the worst case were true, since this is the NSA's classified network. The known impact is an internal outage that kept many staff home; officials say core missions kept running and see no sign of an attack.

Who is affected?

NSA employees, mainly at Fort Meade, Maryland, and a site in Georgia, who could not use the internal network normally and were told to stagger arrivals or stay home. The public is not directly affected and there is nothing the public needs to do.

Known so far

  • The NSA says it had a temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance, and that mission-critical capabilities stayed fully operational. evidence
  • The Director of National Intelligence said patching for recently found PHP flaws caused some NSA systems not to come back up as expected. This confirms what he said, not the technical detail. evidence
  • The Director of National Intelligence says the recent outages were not a cyberattack and not a coordinated nation-state effort. evidence
  • He told a House committee there is no indication of outside or state-sponsored actors behind the NSA disruption. evidence
  • In October, CISA, the FBI and the NSA urged everyone to patch two critical PHP flaws right away. evidence
  • The NSA said on Nov 20 that it is now operating under normal conditions; some facilities had delayed openings that day. evidence

Not known yet

  • Exactly when every NSA facility returned to normal hours. Some were still on delayed openings for non-essential staff on Nov 20.
  • Which systems failed, or what went wrong technically. The NSA has not said.
  • How many staff were affected. CNN reports the outage lasted almost four days.
  • Whether the network first failed on Monday, Nov 16, or Tuesday, Nov 17.
  • Whether the red-team review the Senate Democratic Leader asked for will happen, and what it would find.
  • Whether the NSA or the intelligence community will publish a fuller explanation.
