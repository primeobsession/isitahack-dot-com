Was the Ochsner Health data breach a cyberattack?

Also reported as Ochsner data incident, Ochsner Microsoft 365 breach and Ochsner Vortex claim.

Is it a hack? The short answer

Confirmed

Yes, by Ochsner's own account: an unauthorized party accessed two employee accounts on Sept 18 to 21 and downloaded files from one. How, by whom and how many people are affected are not established.

Rests on Ochsner Health says an unauthorized party accessed two employee user accounts in its Microsoft environment on Sept 18 to 21, 2026, and downloaded files from one of them · Ochsner says it learned of the incident on Oct 1, determined on Nov 18 that specific information was accessed, and began notifying people on Nov 19 · How the two employee accounts were accessed is not established

This is the current answer, not a final verdict. A final verdict is published only after the site's owner has reviewed the evidence.

What we checked and did not find

We looked for these and did not find them as of our latest check on Nov 21.

  • A description of how the two accounts were accessed, or of the attacker, from Ochsner or any regulator.
  • A count of the people affected, from Ochsner, a state attorney general or a federal breach filing. We found no Louisiana or federal filing in the coverage we read.
  • Any statement from Ochsner tying the incident to Vortex, or confirming a ransom demand or publication of data.
  • Any outlet, patient or staff report of a disruption to care or to Epic, MyOchsner or MyChart.
  • A statement from Orlando Health.
  • Independent confirmation of the Vortex file count. We did not look at leak sites, underground forums or trackers, and we cite the claim only through outlets that report it.
  • KLFY's page blocked a direct fetch; its text was read through a search result, not the page itself.
  • A security researcher's report naming Ochsner. Palo Alto Networks Unit 42's Vortex research does not mention Ochsner or Orlando Health.
