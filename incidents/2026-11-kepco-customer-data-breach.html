Was the KEPCO customer data breach a hack?

Also reported as the KEPCO ON leak and KEPCO's second breach.

Is it a hack? The short answer

Confirmed

KEPCO's own notices say an outside cyber intrusion leaked data on about 1.52 million users of its KEPCO ON and SRM systems. How the attacker got in, and who it was, are not established.

Rests on KEPCO says an outside cyber intrusion got into its systems and personal data was leaked · About 1.52 million people's data was leaked across the two systems · KEPCO learned of it on Nov 20, cut outside access and suspended the services

This is the current answer, not a final verdict. A final verdict is published only after the site's owner has reviewed the evidence.

03

How did they get in?

5
Not establishedHow the attacker got inKEPCO has not said how the attacker got in. Its apology says some features offered for customer convenience and access became the target of an outside cyberattack, without naming them.

Why it supports KEPCO's apology names a target area but no method; no cause has been published.

KEPCO's line that attacks are getting more advanced, including with AI, is general, not a finding. Yonhap (Korean) quotes KEPCO saying the attacker appears to have pulled records one at a time and then stopped at some point, which is why only part of KEPCO ON's users were hit; KEPCO says the investigation will tell. We found no cause report. News1's report says the leak came to light through slow access and signs on the dark web; it does not name an entry route. Separately, KEPCO's own spring security review had found serious weaknesses (see c017); nothing public ties them to this breach. Financial News and the Kukmin Daily quote KEPCO saying it became a target as it expanded open digital services such as simplified authentication for checking and paying bills; that names an area, not a method. KEPCO's SRM notice says it blocked the illegal-access IP addresses and patched the related function, without saying what the function was.

What happened?

2
ConfirmedKEPCO learned of it on Nov 20, cut outside access and suspended the servicesKEPCO says it found outside illegal access attempts on Nov 20, blocked them, set up an emergency room, reported to the authorities, asked police to investigate and paused some outside services.

Why it supports KEPCO's own apology and KEPCO ON notice (Tier 1) describe these steps.

Seoul Economic Daily gives KEPCO's timeline: it became aware at 6 AM on Nov 20 (Korea), reported to the climate ministry and the National Intelligence Service at 9:31 AM, and asked the Jeonnam provincial police to investigate at 12:31 PM the same day. Timeline of the KEPCO data breach

9

Times are 2026. Each row opens to show its evidence and source.

Oct 1 Oct 1, 2026 (Korea)KEPCO says about 24,000 employees' data was exposed on an external webpage (the October breach)Reported

KEPCO said on Oct 1 that about 24,000 staff's data was exposed on an outside webpage; by Oct 23 it was 35,528 with retirees and partner staff. Mostly names, affiliations and phones, plus coded face-recognition data of about 400 staff.

Date from Aju Press and Yonhap, relaying KEPCO.

Why it supports Outlets relaying one set of company figures; the CCTV detail rests on KEPCO figures relayed by two outlets, and the Hong Kong detail on one outlet.

Yonhap (Korean) and Aju Press report that KEPCO reported it to the privacy regulator and asked the Jeonnam provincial police to investigate. Both cite figures KEPCO gave a National Assembly committee member's office. KEPCO told a parliamentary audit on Oct 19 that the National Intelligence Service's regional branch alerted it on the afternoon of Oct 1 that staff data was exposed on a webpage hosted with a private cloud provider (Yonhap Oct 19, Today Korea); Segye Ilbo alone says the site was based in Hong Kong. Yonhap (Oct 4) says the page was not open to the general public, the data was removed about 32 hours after KEPCO noticed, and KEPCO said it was unrelated to AI-driven attacks on banks. Segye Ilbo (Oct 22) puts the later addition at 10,759 people: 10,691 retirees and 68 partner-company staff. The Leaves (Oct 20) and Today Korea (Oct 19) put the face-recognition records at 408 and say access details for 14,957 CCTV devices were also exposed; both relay the same KEPCO figures given to a lawmaker's office, and Today Korea adds that KEPCO has no record of how many times the page was viewed or downloaded.
